# Data card: synthetic customer/device decisions

This original deterministic fixture was created for this independent repository in November 2026. Code, synthetic data and original reports use the [MIT license](LICENSE). There are no real customers, employer records, third-party transaction data or purchased services.

One row is one fictional transaction decision. The default seed is 53; 80 decisions arrive each day. Training occupies days [0,30), validation [35,45), and test [50,70): 2,400 / 800 / 1,600 decisions. Customer and device IDs are disjoint across the three cohorts, opaque and excluded from model inputs. Customers' noisy profile attributes are available at their cohort's beginning; latent coordinated membership is never exported or used as a feature.

The mechanism intentionally creates a contextual hypothesis. One quarter of customers can share a small device pool and have a noisier, higher-risk profile distribution. Benign customers sometimes use shared public terminals. Outcomes have random noise plus amount/country/profile effects and a coordinated-customer component. Test day 60 reduces coordinated device reuse and outcome risk while increasing benign public-device use. These generator choices give relational methods an advantage by construction; a favorable result is not independent evidence of a real-world advantage.

`timestamp` and `label_available_at` use synthetic days, not real dates. Positive/negative outcomes become known one to four days after each decision. Training labels must mature by day35, validation labels by day50 and test labels by day75. Label, maturity time and latent mechanisms never enter graph/node/transaction features. No outcome-derived edges exist.

Graph snapshots use exactly [day-7,day) at the beginning of a decision day. Event ingestion is assumed in order and immediate; late arrivals/retractions are not modeled. Within-day events are deliberately unavailable until the next midnight. Only prior devices are materialized; a new device gets an isolated type-only default node. All 120 customer profiles are pre-provisioned per cohort and known at cohort start, including customers that have not yet transacted. Their future edges are absent.

Exact duplicate event retries are removed; conflicting retries or entities crossing cohort boundaries are rejected. Decision times, label arrival times and label as-of boundaries must be finite real numbers; an unknown/nonfinite arrival never counts as a mature label. A label is available exactly at its declared arrival time. `results/data/transactions.csv.gz` contains the full generated audit fixture, `profiles.json` contains known attributes, and `results/metrics.json` records decompressed CSV, gzip and profile hashes. Labels are included for reproducibility/audit, not exposed to the feature builder. Daily capacity is an offline whole-day ranking at floor(5% × daily decisions), which is not a live arrival/review simulator.

The fixture omits real prevalence, chargeback disputes, intervention effects, consent and identity resolution, demographics, label error, staffing economics and adversarial adaptation. Rankings are uncalibrated; they do not authorize decisions about real people. A representative authorized dataset and monitored shadow evaluation would be needed for any operational proposal.
